In the last post, we covered how you get enrolled in National Health Insurance and what it costs. The part that actually trips people up isn't the enrollment — it's what happens afterward: a missed payment, a long trip home, or a change of address.

**Quick answer:** Missing a payment suspends your coverage from the first day of the second month after the deadline, and unpaid premiums are shared with immigration and can affect your ability to extend your stay. Leaving Korea for more than a month also ends your coverage, though it resumes automatically the day you re-enter if your visa is still valid.

## If You Fall Behind on Payments

- **Late fees.** Missing the deadline adds a penalty of up to 5%.
- **Coverage stops.** If you fall behind, your benefits are suspended from the first day of the second month after the missed deadline, until you pay in full.
- **No retroactive refund.** Paying later doesn't reimburse what you spent on treatment while you were in arrears.
- **Immigration is notified.** Arrears data is shared with the Ministry of Justice and can restrict your permission to stay.

This catches exchange students in two specific moments: applying to extend your stay for a second semester, and returning to Korea later for a degree or a job. If you're planning to stay another semester, check your payment record on the NHIS website or by calling 1577-1000 about a month beforehand — finding a problem at the immigration counter leaves you no time to fix it.

## When Your Coverage Ends

Coverage ends in two situations: your permitted stay expires, or you leave Korea for more than one month. That second one matters when you're planning holidays — a trip home longer than a month ends your coverage while you're away. If your student visa is still valid, it resumes automatically the day you re-enter Korea. If a trip will run past a month, call NHIS before you go to ask how your premiums will be handled in the meantime.

## If You Move Apartments

Your bills go to the address on your Residence Card. If you move — from a dormitory to a studio, say — report the change to an immigration office or district office within 14 days. Arrears caused by a bill going to your old address are still your responsibility, so this one is easy to lose track of during a move.

## Before You Fly Home for Good

Since premiums are paid in advance, you've likely already covered your last month — but check with NHIS for any remaining balance and settle it before you leave. If you're on automatic debit, keep the account open until the final collection goes through. Unpaid amounts stay on record and follow you back if you return to Korea later.

## Applying for Exclusion (Realistically, Most Students Won't Qualify)

If a foreign law, a foreign insurance policy, or an employment contract already gives you medical cover equivalent to NHIS, you can apply for exclusion under Article 109 of the National Health Insurance Act. In practice, the bar is high: your policy needs no cap on amount, scope, or frequency, cover of at least ₩1 billion, a start date before your alien registration, and — for women — maternity cover, without which exclusion is refused outright. Every supporting document also needs a Korean translation attached, or it simply won't be processed.

A one-semester travel policy or standard student insurance plan almost never meets this bar. For most exchange students, paying into NHIS rather than pursuing an exclusion is the realistic path.

## Quick Answers to Common Questions

- **My bill never arrived — do I still owe it?** Yes. Call NHIS, get a virtual account number, and pay — then set up electronic billing or automatic debit so it doesn't happen again.
- **I have no Korean bank account yet — how do I pay?** Start with a virtual account transfer or a convenience store payment, then switch to automatic debit once your account is open.
- **A bill arrived after I'd already left Korea.** Contact NHIS about paying from abroad, or through someone still in Korea — left unpaid, it becomes arrears that surfaces the next time you enter the country.

## The Bottom Line

The system runs quietly in the background as long as you keep two things current: your payment (due the 25th of the month before) and your address (reported within 14 days of any move). Miss either one and the consequences show up later, usually at the worst possible moment — an immigration counter or a hospital reception desk.
