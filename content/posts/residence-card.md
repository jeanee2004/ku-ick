In the last post, we covered getting your visa sorted before you left home. Now that you've landed, there's one more official step between you and a fully functional Korean life: registering as a foreign resident.

This card — officially called the **Residence Card**, though you'll still hear people call it the ARC out of habit — is your Korean ID for the length of your stay. Until you have it, ordinary things like opening a bank account or signing a phone contract simply aren't possible.

Here's exactly how to get it, from booking your appointment to picking up the finished card.

**Quick answer:** Within 90 days of entering Korea, book an appointment through HiKorea, visit your local immigration office with your documents and a 35,000 KRW fee, and give your fingerprints. Processing takes about 4–6 weeks, after which you can pick up your Residence Card in person or have it mailed.

## Why This Card Matters

Inside Korea, the Residence Card carries the same weight as a national ID card does for Korean citizens. Without it, you can't open a local bank account, register a mobile phone line in your own name, or enroll in National Health Insurance — so it really is the single most important document you'll get during your first months here.

## Step 1 — Book Your HiKorea Appointment

Go to **hikorea.go.kr** and use the **Reserve Visit** menu. If it's your first time and you don't have a Residence Card yet, you can book as a non-member using your passport information rather than creating a full account. Choose the immigration office that has jurisdiction over your Korean address, then pick an available date — reservations open starting the day after you request, and same-day booking isn't allowed. Print or save your confirmation with the date, time, and window number.

## Step 2 — Gather Your Documents and Attend

Bring the following to your appointment:

- Completed integrated application form (available at the office, or printable from HiKorea in advance)
- Original passport, plus a photocopy
- Your printed Visa Grant Notice from the visa step
- One color photo, 3.5cm × 4.5cm
- Proof of your Korean address, such as a lease agreement
- A processing fee of **35,000 KRW** (recently raised from 30,000 KRW to cover the new IC-chip cards)

You'll also have your fingerprints taken as part of standard biometric registration — routine, nothing to worry about.

## Step 3 — Wait for Processing and Collect Your Card

Processing usually takes about 4 to 6 weeks. You can pick up the physical card in person once it's ready, or pay a small extra fee to have it mailed to you. Once you're holding it, you can also apply for the mobile version of your Residence Card through Korea's official Mobile ID system — a digital copy with the same legal effect, handy for daily use once your physical card is issued.

## Tips & Precautions

- **Book your appointment immediately, especially in March or September.** These months line up with the start of Korean university semesters, so immigration offices get busy and slots fill up fast — try to book within your first week if you're arriving during one of these periods.
- **Report any address change within 14 days.** If you move apartments later, update your registered address through the HiKorea e-Application system or your local district office — skipping this can lead to a fine.
- **Keep your name spelling and spacing identical to your visa documents.** This exact layout carries forward into your phone contract and bank account too, so it's worth a careful check before you submit anything.
- **Use HiKorea's e-Application for simple changes later on.** Extending your stay or changing your workplace can often be handled entirely online, without a return trip to the immigration office.

## The Bottom Line

Once this card is in your hands, the rest of settling into Korea moves fast — banking, phone service, health insurance, and housing all become possible.
