If you followed our earlier guide and opened a temporary line with just your passport, there's one more errand once your Residence Card arrives: getting your carrier to actually put the card's details on file. Your number and SIM don't change — but until this is done, your phone account is invisible to the banks, offices, and apps that check for it.

**Quick answer:** Your carrier's subscriber record needs three things from your Residence Card on file: your Alien Registration Number, the card's issue date, and your nationality. If you opened your line with the card already in hand, this happened automatically. If you opened on a passport first, you need to go back and have it added — your number stays the same.

## Which Situation Are You In?

- **Card in hand, no line yet:** just open your line now — the details get recorded as part of setup, so there's nothing to come back for.
- **Opened a line on a passport before the card arrived:** this is the common case for exchange students. The card details need to be added to your existing account — covered below.
- **Your line is in a friend's or employer's name:** a Residence Card can't be attached to someone else's account. You'll need a line in your own name.

## Adding Your Card to an Existing Line

- Collect your Residence Card.
- Contact your carrier and ask what they need to update the record — the process differs slightly between operators, so a quick call first can save a wasted trip.
- Bring your passport and Residence Card, and ask them to record your Alien Registration Number, the card's issue date, and your nationality on the account.
- Before anything is saved, check that the name and number on screen match your card exactly.
- Ask them to confirm the record was updated before you leave.

Your number and USIM don't change through any of this — only the subscriber record behind the scenes gets updated.

## Keeping the Record Current Afterward

- **If you extend your stay:** tell your carrier — your contract is managed against your permitted period of stay.
- **If your card is reissued:** have the record updated with the new issue date.
- **Before you leave Korea:** close the line or agree with your carrier how it will be handled. An unpaid balance follows you and causes problems later.

## Quick Answers to Common Questions

- **Do I need a new number once my card arrives?** No — the number and USIM stay as they are; only the card details are added.
- **Can this be done online or by phone?** It varies by carrier, so ask yours directly.
- **How do I confirm it actually worked?** Call your carrier's customer service line and ask them to check whether your Alien Registration Number is on file for your account.

## The Bottom Line

This is a five-minute follow-up, not a new phone contract — but it's an easy one to forget, since your phone keeps working fine either way. Do it as soon as your Residence Card arrives, before you need to prove your identity through your phone number for something time-sensitive like a bank account.
