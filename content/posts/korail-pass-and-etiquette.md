If you're covering long distances across several separate days, the **KORAIL Pass** — a flat-rate rail pass sold only to foreign visitors — is worth pricing out. A single Seoul–Busan round trip doesn't justify it, but a week hopping between Seoul, Busan, Gwangju, and Jeonju easily does.

**Quick answer:** The KORAIL Pass lets any non-Korean visitor travel by train at a flat price, chosen from a set number of travel days within a 10-day window. It excludes the metropolitan subway and temporary tourist trains; whether former SRT routes are now included is still being finalized following the September 2026 operator merger, so check korail.com before relying on that detail.

## What the Pass Costs

Passes are sold as "select" passes — you choose your travel days within a 10-day window starting from the pass's start date. Adult pricing applies from age 13; children ages 6–12 pay half, and one child under 6 travels free with each adult.

- 2-day select pass: ₩131,000 adult / ₩66,000 child
- 3-day select pass: ₩186,000 adult / ₩93,000 child
- 4-day select pass: ₩234,000 adult / ₩117,000 child
- 5-day select pass: ₩275,000 adult / ₩138,000 child

## Buying and Using the Pass

- Buy it with a valid passport and a credit card.
- Reserve seats under **My Ticket → Seat Reservation** — reservations open one month before departure, and you can hold up to two tickets per person per day.
- Board carrying the pass, your passport, and your seat reservation if you made one.

If a train is sold out, pass holders may still board and stand with a valid pass and passport. First class is available at half the normal first-class fare for pass holders, though the same journey can't be booked twice on one date — cancel the existing reservation first if you want to switch departures.

## Conditions Worth Knowing

- **The pass is non-transferable.** It belongs to the named holder only — selling or lending it is illegal and carries penalties.
- **A screenshot isn't a valid ticket.** The pass or seat reservation has to be shown live in your phone's browser.
- **Refunds only work before your pass starts.** Once the validity window begins, nothing is refundable, and delays or cancellations aren't compensated for pass holders.
- **Misuse is expensive.** Improper use draws the standard fare plus a surcharge of 10 to 30 times that fare.

## Riding Like a Local: Train Etiquette

- **Nobody checks your ticket.** There are no ticket gates — just find your car and seat number. Inspections do happen, so this isn't an invitation to skip paying.
- **Trains are quiet.** Conversation stays low, and calls are taken in the vestibule between cars, not at your seat. Use headphones for anything with audio.
- **Take your assigned seat.** Seats are individually reserved, with the car and seat number printed on your ticket — don't move somewhere that looks emptier. Some rows face backward relative to travel direction; that's normal, not a booking error.
- **Eating on board is completely normal.** Anything sold at the station — gimbap, fried chicken, coffee, even beer — is fair game on the train. Just take your rubbish with you.
- **Leave priority seats free.** Seats nearest the doors are reserved for elderly, disabled, and pregnant passengers. Large suitcases go in the racks at the ends of each car, not the aisle.

## The Bottom Line

Price the KORAIL Pass against buying individual tickets before a multi-city trip — it's rarely worth it for a single round trip, but pays off fast once you're covering three or more cities in a week. Either way, once you're on the platform, the culture is simple: be early, stay quiet, and sit where your ticket says.
