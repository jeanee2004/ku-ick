Before you think about apartments, SIM cards, or anything else, there's one thing that has to happen first: your visa. Get this step right and everything downstream — the flight, the move-in, registering as a resident — falls into place on schedule.

If you're staying in Korea for more than 90 days, on a D-2 student visa, D-4 general trainee, E-2 language instructor, E-7, or an F-series residency visa, this applies to you. It's genuinely the first item on your Korea to-do list, and it has to happen before you leave home.

This post walks through exactly what to do, and what to hold onto once you're approved. The second milestone — registering as a foreign resident once you land — is in the next post.

**Quick answer:** If you're staying more than 90 days, apply for the right visa category through the Korea Visa Portal or a Korean embassy or consulate in your home country before you fly. Once approved, download and print your Visa Grant Notice — you'll need the physical copy at immigration when you land, and again when you register as a resident.

## Who This Applies To

The rule is simple: anyone planning to stay longer than 90 days needs to apply for the matching visa category before departure — D-2 for degree students, D-4 for general trainees and language programs, E-2 for language instructors, E-7 for specific professions, or an F-series visa for longer-term residency. Whatever your situation, the visa category has to match your actual purpose of stay, so double-check before you apply.

## Step 1 — Choose Your Visa Category and Apply

You can apply at a Korean embassy or consulate in your home country, or file electronically through the **Korea Visa Portal** at visa.go.kr. Some countries also have an authorized Korea Visa Application Center (VAC) — a third-party office that processes applications on the embassy's behalf. If you're applying from Europe, for example, the KVAC network handles this; check whether your country has one, since certain visa categories must go through a VAC rather than the embassy directly.

One safety note worth taking seriously: there are copycat websites designed to look like the real visa portal. The only official site is visa.go.kr — bookmark it directly rather than clicking a link from a search result or a message.

## Step 2 — Download and Print Your Visa Grant Notice

Once your visa is approved, log back into the Visa Portal and download your **Visa Grant Notice**. Print a physical copy and keep it with your passport. You'll need to show it at immigration when you arrive, and again later when you register as a resident, so it's worth keeping somewhere safe and easy to find rather than buried in your luggage.

## Step 3 — Pack Both Documents for the Flight

Keep your visa (or e-visa notice) and your printed Visa Grant Notice in your carry-on, not checked luggage. Immigration officers ask for these on arrival, and scrambling through a suitcase at the airport is not how you want to start your time in Korea.

## Tips & Precautions

- **Apply well ahead of your program start date.** Processing takes time, and visa officers sometimes request additional documents — don't book flights until your visa is actually approved.
- **Match your name exactly, everywhere, starting now.** The English spelling, spacing, and order of your name on this application should be identical across your passport, Visa Grant Notice, Residence Card, and eventually your Korean phone contract. A missing space can cause frustrating system errors months later.
- **Only trust the official visa portal.** Bookmark visa.go.kr directly and avoid visa sites that show up in ads or unsolicited messages.
- **Confirm your visa category still fits if your plans change.** A change in school, program, or length of stay can mean you need a different visa category — check before assuming your existing approval still applies.

## The Bottom Line

Getting your visa sorted from home means you land in Korea with the hardest part already behind you. The next step — registering as a foreign resident and getting your Residence Card — happens within your first 90 days here, and that's exactly what the next post covers.
